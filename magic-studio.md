## From a product photo to a campaign visual

Three steps, and every one of them can be run again on its own.

1. **Drop in what you have** — A product photo from your computer, from Drive or from the brand's own pictures, and a prompt — as rough as you like. Each can be a node of its own, or a reference on the image step itself.
2. **Wire the steps** — Wire the prompt into Generate or a photo into Edit Image, pick a model for each step, and set the ratio, resolution, quality and how many to make.
3. **Rerun until it is right** — Re-generate lands beside the last result instead of over it, so every attempt stays on the canvas. Cut the background out, edit a result further, or download it.

## Built for images you will need again

Not a prompt box — a canvas that remembers how every picture was made.

### Twenty-five models on one canvas

GPT Image, Seedream, FLUX.2, Nano Banana, Qwen Image, Ideogram, Recraft and more — each step picks its own, so comparing two models is two nodes side by side rather than two browser tabs and a spreadsheet.

### Your products, on-model

Every generator step carries a Brand. Name one of its products in the prompt and the packshots you uploaded come along as references, so the bottle on the poster is your bottle.

### A prompt writer that knows the brand

Write a rough note and the art-director agent turns it into a proper English prompt from your brand profile and catalogue, and marks which products it is about. It lands in the prompt box before anything is generated, ready to edit.

### Edit with up to 16 references

Change what is in an image with a sentence and a handful of references — the product, the setting, the model, the palette — and keep the source's size when it matters.

### Every attempt kept, cut-outs included

Re-generate adds a new node beside the last one with the same settings, so earlier results stay to compare. Remove the background of a result — the cut-out is added next to the original, ready for packshots and marketplace listings.

### Made together, traceable later

Your team edits the same canvas in real time, and every image keeps a record of the steps and sources it came from — so the one that worked can be made again.

## Questions about Magic Studio

What teams ask before they move their product shots over.

### Which image models can I use?

Twenty-five, from eleven makers: GPT Image 2 and 2.5 from OpenAI, Seedream from ByteDance, FLUX.2 and Flux from Black Forest Labs, Nano Banana from Google, Qwen Image from Alibaba, Ideogram, Recraft, HiDream, Krea, Bria FIBO — trained on licensed data — and ImagineArt. Each generator step on the canvas picks its own model, so one canvas can compare several.

### Do I need an account with each model provider?

No. Every model runs through the platform, so there are no provider keys to collect and no separate bills. Generation is counted as usage on your workspace, like the rest of the AI you use here.

### Will my product still look like my product?

Each generator step carries a Brand. When the prompt names one of that brand's products, its reference pictures — the packshots you uploaded — are attached to the request automatically, so the model works from the real item instead of guessing at it. Edit Image takes up to 16 reference images of its own for everything else.

### I am not good at writing prompts. Does that matter?

Less than you would think. Write a rough note and the art-director agent rewrites it into a full prompt from the brand profile and the product catalogue — in English, which image models read best — and marks which products it is about. The rewrite goes straight into the prompt box before anything is generated, so you can still edit it or write over it.

### Can it make video?

Video lives in Storyboard, where a shot has the camera, the action and the characters it needs to be rendered well. Panels drawn there use the same image models as Magic Studio.

### Can my team work on the same canvas?

Yes — canvases are edited together in real time, and every image keeps a record of the steps and sources it came from. Finished images can be downloaded, or picked from the image library for a post on the content calendar.
