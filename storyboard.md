## From a page of script to a cut

The pre-production half of a video, and the render at the end of it.

1. **Write it from a brief** — A Content Calendar video slot — or a chat with your agent — becomes scenes with their sluglines and shots inside each, every shot with its camera, action and the lines that are spoken.
2. **Cast it and draw it** — Add the characters, products and locations that recur, pick a style, and draw every panel — one at a time or the whole board in a batch.
3. **Render the clips** — Render a shot, or a whole scene as one clip, with the cost shown before you start. Every take is kept, so the first version is never lost to the second.

## Pre-production that renders

A storyboard that stops being a PDF the moment the shots are drawn.

### Scenes, shots and keyframes

The structure a director already thinks in. A shot is one panel and one four-to-ten-second clip, with the camera size, angle, lens and up to three moves, the action, what moves in the take with its effects and sounds, voice-over and dialogue on it.

### Characters that stay on-model

Elements hold the characters, products, locations and props that recur, each with its own references. An element sheet draws every angle at once, Looks change an outfit per scene, and a product can link to its row in your catalogue.

### Seven looks, one board

Cinematic, photoreal, anime, comic book, storyboard sketch, 3D render or watercolour — drawn with the same image models as Magic Studio, with your Brand applied.

### A clip per shot, or per scene

Kling 2.5 Turbo Pro and Seedance 2.5, from a prompt or from a first and last frame; Seedance up to 1080p with its own sound. Seedance can render a whole scene as one multi-shot clip from up to thirty references.

### The cost before the render

The render plan shows the estimated cost of what you are about to render, before anything is spent — so a thirty-second scene is a decision, not a surprise on the invoice.

### Every take kept

Each render of a shot or scene is kept as a take you can restore. Try the bolder camera move; the safe version is still one click away.

## Questions about Storyboard

What teams ask before they plan their next video here.

### How is a storyboard organised?

As a film is: scenes with a slugline (INT. KITCHEN – DAY), shots inside each scene — one panel and one clip of four to ten seconds — and keyframes inside a shot, each drawn from the one before it. A shot carries its camera (size, angle, lens and up to three moves), the action, the subject's motion with its effects and sounds, voice-over and dialogue.

### How does it keep a character looking the same in every shot?

Through Elements — the characters, products, locations and props that recur. Each has its own reference pictures, a product can be linked to its row in your catalogue, and an element sheet draws every angle in one image. Looks let the same character change outfit scene by scene without becoming someone else.

### Which video models does it render with?

Kling 2.5 Turbo Pro, from a prompt or from a first and last frame, in five or ten seconds; and Seedance 2.5, from four to thirty seconds with its own sound. Seedance can also render a whole scene as one multi-shot clip from up to thirty reference images, at 480p, 720p or 1080p. A shot's length is rounded up to one the model renders and trimmed in the edit.

### Will I know what a render costs before I start it?

Yes. The render plan shows the estimated cost of what you are about to render before anything is spent, and every finished render is kept as a take — so trying a second version never throws away the first.

### Do I have to write the prompts myself?

No. Panels are drawn from the shot's own fields, in one of seven styles, and a whole storyboard can be drawn in one batch. For video, the prompt is built from the shot — its camera, action, who is in frame and what is said — and shown, read-only, before you render. Anything the fields cannot say goes in a note, which the Art Director can write for you.

### Can my team and my client see it?

Your team edits the storyboard together in real time, with presence. For anyone outside the workspace there is a public share link and a print view, in grid, list or script layout.
