## From a brand brief to a published post

One pipeline, with the approval gate where you would want it.

1. **Set up a Brand** — Describe the voice, the audience and the products once, then connect the destinations it publishes to. Everything downstream plans against that brand.
2. **Fill the calendar** — Ask for a week or a quarter of ideas and place them into slots on the grid. Drag to reschedule, re-roll the fields that miss, and write the ones you would rather write yourself.
3. **Sign off and publish** — Ask for a status your Brand gates and the request waits in the approver's queue. Press Publish and the post goes to every destination attached, and the log records what went where.

## The whole content pipeline in one place

Planning, writing, visuals, review and delivery — not four tools and a spreadsheet between them.

### Plans that know the brand

A Brand holds the voice, the audience, the products and the rules. Every idea is planned against it, so the calendar fills with content that sounds like you rather than like a prompt.

### Re-roll one field, not the whole idea

When the angle is right but the headline is flat, regenerate just the headline. The parts you already approved stay exactly as they were instead of being lost to another full pass.

### Visuals from the same brief

Brief the pictures a post needs — or let the writer brief them — and generate them one at a time inside the post; each finished image lands on it by itself. When one needs more work, open it in Magic Studio.

### Sign-off on the statuses that matter

A Brand names its own statuses and decides which of them need sign-off, and from whom. Asking for one sends a request to the approver's queue; the post keeps its status until every signature is in, and a rejection says what to change.

### Publish to more than one place

Facebook Pages and WordPress natively, anything else through a webhook destination your own system receives. A Brand can hold several, and one piece can go to all of them.

### A publish log you can trust

Every attempt is recorded with its destination, its result and the live URL. A post that failed to go out is visible in the log rather than quietly missing from the feed.

## Questions about the content calendar

What marketing teams ask before they move a quarter of planning over.

### Where do the content ideas come from?

From a Brand — the voice, audience, products and rules you configure once. An agent plans against that brand rather than against a blank prompt, so the ideas arrive already on-topic. Any single field of an idea can be re-rolled on its own when the angle is right but the headline is not, instead of regenerating the whole topic and losing the parts you liked.

### Where can it publish?

Facebook Pages and WordPress sites directly, and anywhere else through the Custom API destination — a webhook your own system receives, which is how teams push into a CMS or a scheduler we do not integrate with natively. Each Brand can hold several destinations, and one piece of content can go to more than one.

### Does anything publish without a human seeing it?

No. A date on the calendar only places a post — nothing goes out until someone presses Publish on its destinations. Sign-off, where your Brand asks for it, sits on the status: the request waits for its approvers before the post can move there. Every publish attempt is recorded with the destination, the result and the live URL, so a post that failed to go out is visible rather than silently missing.

### Can it make the images too?

Yes. The pictures a post goes out with are briefed — by you, or by the writer as it drafts — and generated one at a time inside the post, and each finished image lands on the post, stored on a durable URL rather than a link that expires in a week. When one needs more work, open it in Magic Studio.

### Does it handle video scripts?

Script storyboards break a script into scenes you can reorder, illustrate and share by link — the planning half of a video, kept next to the rest of the campaign instead of in a separate document nobody can find.

### What happens to content after it is published?

It stays in the Drive alongside your documents, mind maps and tables, which is the same knowledge base your AI agents read. A campaign you wrote in March is something your support agent can quote in June.
